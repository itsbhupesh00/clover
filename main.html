SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH · 2013

Korolev: A 2-km-thick Ice Dome

A detailed SHARAD radar investigation by T. C. Brothers and J. W. Holt (University of Texas) reconstructed the internal stratigraphy of Korolev Crater on Mars.

Key Findings

  • Ice depth: Subsurface radar reflectors extend to approximately 1.8 km beneath the surface.
  • Ice volume: Conservative estimates indicate approximately 1,580 km³ of ice-rich material.
  • Layer structure: Multiple irregularly spaced radar reflectors reveal alternating ice-rich and reflector-poor zones.
  • Climate connection: Korolev's upper ice layers show similarities to the North Polar Layered Deposits (NPLD), suggesting a shared regional climate signal.
  • Local influences: Solar ablation thins and tilts ice layers on south-facing slopes, while deposition is stronger near the northern crater wall.

Research Significance

The findings support atmospheric deposition and localized, relatively steady growth of Korolev's ice dome rather than accumulation from a larger former ice sheet. Its layered structure preserves evidence of past Martian climate conditions.

Source: Brothers & Holt, 44th Lunar and Planetary Science Conference (2013), Abstract 3022.
